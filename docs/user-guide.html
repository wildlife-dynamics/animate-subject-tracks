Animate Subject Tracks Workflow — User Guide

This guide walks you through loading, configuring, and running the Animate Subject Tracks Workflow, which turns an EarthRanger subject group's GPS history into an animated 3D map, and optionally an MP4 video.

What it produces

  • An interactive animated 3D map (animated_map.html) with a playback bar, map buttons (zoom, compass, reset view, fullscreen), a legend of subject names, and a Camera panel for designing how the camera moves
  • An optional MP4 video (animation.mp4) of the same animation, recorded with the same camera the map plays with. Video export is off by default.
  • GeoParquet files of the cleaned relocations and trajectories
  • A dashboard showing the animated map, with a Files section for downloading the map and the video

Requirements

  • An EarthRanger connection configured in the workflow runner
  • A subject group in EarthRanger, e.g. Elephants
  • Internet access while the workflow runs and while you view the map, for terrain elevation and basemap imagery tiles

Longer time ranges and larger subject groups take longer to fetch and, if video export is on, to record as video. For a first run, a range of a few days to a few weeks works well.

1. Load the Workflow

In the workflow runner, go to Workflow Templates and click Add Workflow Template. Paste this repository's URL into the Github Link field, then click Add Template:

https://github.com/wildlife-dynamics/animate-subject-tracks.git

Once added, the workflow appears in the Workflow Templates list. Click it to open the configuration form.

The card may show Initializing… briefly while the environment is set up.

2. Configure the Workflow

Only the Workflow Name, Data Source, Time Range and Subject Group Name need to be filled in. Every other section is pre-filled with defaults and can be left alone on a first run. Fields marked advanced are collapsed on the form.

Trajectory Segment Filter (advanced)

Removes GPS noise by dropping track segments whose length, duration or speed falls outside these limits. The defaults suit most terrestrial wildlife.

LimitMinimumMaximum
Segment length0.001 m100 000 m

Terrain Exaggeration (advanced)

FieldDefaultDescription
Exaggeration1.0Vertical scale of the 3D terrain. 1.0 is true scale; 2.0 doubles apparent heights, which helps on flat landscapes. The tracks are raised to match.

Terrain Sampling (advanced)

Track height sets where the tracks are drawn. The choices follow Google Maps 3D's altitude modes, and each shows only its own setting:

OptionSettingWhat it does
Above the ground (default)Height 30 mTracks float a set height above the ground
On the ground—Tracks lie on the ground; any recorded altitude is ignored
Recorded altitude (where available)Height without an altitude 30 mEach fix at its recorded altitude above sea level. Fixes without one (usual for EarthRanger data) sit the set height above the ground instead of at sea level.
Above the 3D terrainHeight 30 mA set height above the terrain as drawn on the map, so tracks, markers and labels stay on the surface at every zoom

Tracks also get an extra point every 150 m, so lines follow ridges and valleys between fixes instead of cutting through hills.

Camera

FieldDefaultDescription
Camera settingsA fixed view of the whole sceneSettings copied from the map's Camera panel (or the path of its downloaded .json file). The interactive map plays with this camera, and so does the video.

To set a camera:

  1. Run the workflow once and open the animated map.
  2. Click the camera button on the playback bar, pick a camera and adjust it. Press play to preview it; Use current view fills in the centre, distance, tilt and heading from wherever you've moved the map.
  3. Click Copy settings (or Download), paste them into Camera settings, and run the workflow again.
CameraWhat it does
Fixed viewHolds one view for the whole clip; blank values frame the whole scene
Fly to & aroundFlies in from the whole scene, then circles a point (blank = the centre of the data)
Follow a subjectStays behind one subject, facing where it is heading (blank = the longest-running track)
Subject tourFollows each subject in turn, flying between them, with each subject's name shown as the camera reaches it
Follow the actionFrames the recent movement of all subjects (or the ones you list), re-fitting as they move and spread
Cinematic fly-throughOpens on the whole scene, flies in to the action, then follows it with a tilted camera that slowly turns
KeyframesFlies through waypoints you add in the panel, upload as a file (.json, .geojson, .csv or .tsv with lon/lat), or derive from a subject's track

The following cameras (Follow a subject, Subject tour, Follow the action, Cinematic fly-through) move smoothly and also offer Lead room (space ahead of moving subjects), Zoom with the action (in while subjects rest, out while they travel fast) and Keep in view over hills (the camera rises when a ridge would hide what it follows).

Video Export

Exports the animation as an MP4 video. Video export is off by default, so only the interactive map is produced.

FieldDefaultDescription
Export videoOffTurn on to also render the animation as an MP4 video. Recording a video makes the workflow take longer to run.

Video Settings (advanced) only apply when Export video is on. The video uses the camera set in Animation → Camera.

FieldDefaultDescription
DurationAutoAuto matches the animation's playback length. Uncheck it to set a fixed length in seconds.
OverlaysAll onThe date and time (and which corner it sits in), legend, north arrow, scale bar, title and marker labels
Resolution720p720p, 1080p or 4K landscape, 720p or 1080p vertical (9:16), 1080p square (1:1), or a custom width and height

3. Run the Workflow

Click Submit. The workflow fetches the observations, cleans them into trajectories, drapes them on the terrain and builds the animated map. If Export video is on, it then records the video frame by frame in a headless browser; otherwise that step is skipped.

When it's on, recording the video is usually the slowest step. The default settings give 900 frames (30 seconds at 30 fps). Higher resolutions and longer durations take proportionally longer.

4. Outputs

All files are written to the workflow's results folder (ECOSCOPE_WORKFLOWS_RESULTS). On ecoscope-web this is cloud storage; the video is recorded locally and uploaded there.

FileDescription
animated_map.htmlThe interactive animated 3D map, shown on the dashboard as Subject movements
animation.mp4H.264 video of the animation. Only written when Export video is on.
relocations_<hash>.geoparquetCleaned GPS fixes
trajectories_<hash>.geoparquetTrajectory segments after the segment filter

The dashboard shows the Subject movements map with the workflow name, description and time range. Its Files section lists animated_map.html and, when exported, animation.mp4.
